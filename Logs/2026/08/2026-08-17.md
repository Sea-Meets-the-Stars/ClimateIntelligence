### Narrative

The work.md prompt doc had been reorganized (by the author) into "Prep" and
"Calculations" subsections under both Prompts and Q&A. The first Calculations
prompt reads: "Ok, I've answered the latest round of questions in Q&A. Please
read them. Then proceed to do your first round of calculations. Log your
work. Use Opus. If questions arise during your work, ask them in the Q&A/
Calculations section below." Re-read the full file, including the round-3
Q&A answers that had come in since the last turn: the eldercare intensity
ramp was confirmed, disabled working-age adults were confirmed to generate
care demand (with a surprising and welcome addition — active 60-70-year-old
retirees can supply, not just consume, non-traditional labor for roughly
their first decade of retirement), and the "sufficiency" standard-of-living
principle was confirmed with no anchors moved.

This was the first turn in the whole work.md sequence that required actually
producing numbers rather than more questions, and the model specified across
three Q&A rounds turned out to need a lot of empirical grounding: shelter
labor-hours per dwelling, food-system labor-hours, nursing-home staffing
ratios, K-12 staffing ratios, and BLS sector-employment figures for clothing/
furniture/utilities/freight, none of which existed anywhere in the project's
context files. Rather than research all of this inline, specified a precise
demographic/computational model myself first — an idealized steady-state
80-year life cycle with explicit tiers (children 0-19, students 20-24,
working adults 25-59 with a 9.05% non-working-disabled carve-out, active
elders 60-69 who are net LABOR CONTRIBUTORS rather than pure dependents,
frail elders 70-74, institutional-care elders 75-79) — so that four parallel
Opus research agents could each fill in empirical values against a fixed
target rather than inventing their own framings. Dispatched shelter, food,
care/disability/medical, and education/goods/infrastructure research in
parallel, plus two direct web searches for population and life-expectancy/
fertility data.

The research came back extraordinarily thorough — each agent independently
pulled primary sources (CMS Payroll-Based Journal nursing-home data, NCES
staffing tables, BLS CES/OEWS/ECEC series via the public API, USDA ERS food-
system labor data, Census ASPEP public-utility employment, the BTS/FHWA
Freight Analysis Framework for essential-vs-discretionary freight shares) and
flagged its own soft numbers versus solid ones throughout. Two of the four
agents apparently spawned their own follow-up sub-agents for deeper dives
(home-maintenance/DIY-hours research and JCHS remodeling-spending research)
that arrived as separate task notifications after the main batch — a sign of
how much latitude a full-tool-access subagent has, worth remembering for
future orchestration.

Handed all of this to a final Opus agent tasked with the actual population
arithmetic and writing `CI_Reports/work_hours_needed.py`. That agent was
interrupted TWICE by an unrelated local machine-sleep issue (an environment
problem, not a task failure) — resumed it via SendMessage each time. By the
second interruption the file already existed complete and correct on disk;
verified this directly rather than assuming from the notification's word
"failed," which in this case did not mean the work was lost. Ran the script

The result: 46.0 hours/week per healthy working-age adult at CENTRAL (9.1

most important finding wasn't the headline number itself but something the

dominate the non-traditional total — childcare and general household tasks,
together ~70% of non-traditional demand and ~58% of the ENTIRE model — were
the only two categories NOT given a dedicated needs-based research pass this
round; they're raw ATUS behavioral benchmarks standing in for the "needs-
based, independent of current practice" estimate the author had actually
asked for. Surfaced this, plus three other genuine forks (whether medical
care is traditional or non-traditional per the activity-classification rule;
which convention to use for the elder labor-contribution scenario alignment;
and a sanity-check flag on clothing's unexpectedly large share of traditional
hours, driven by counting offshore-embodied labor for the ~97%-imported US
apparel supply) as a fourth round of Q&A questions, rather than silently
picking defaults for choices this consequential. Added the script's headline
results, category breakdowns, and these four questions to `work.md`'s Report
and Q&A/Calculations sections respectively.
