Rather than answer from general knowledge, and per the CLAUDE.md rule that

`blogs/blog001/calc_fig4_emissions_definitions.py`. It reuses the cached
OWID/GCP world series and the NOAA Mauna Loa monthly file already in the
repo (no new downloads) and fits doubling times for five definitions:
fossil-only, fossil + land-use change, all-GHG CO2-eq, cumulative CO2, and
CO2 retained in the atmosphere above 280 ppm. It also computes the decadal
airborne fraction as the observational test of "crippled sinks," and draws a
two-panel comparison figure (`fig4_alt_emissions_definitions.png`: annual
flows left, stocks right) in the same lighter blog style as Figure 4.

One mistake caught on the first run: the retained-CO2 series starts in
1959, so the table reported an "1850–1970" doubling time for it that was
really fit to 12 years of data. Added a guard that returns NaN when a series
does not cover the requested window, and re-ran.

Findings, written into the Comments/Figure 4 section: Figure 4 should be
relabelled "fossil-fuel CO2." Adding land-use change weakens the historical
exponential (44-yr vs 22-yr doubling) because deforestation dominated before
1900. Feedback sources are not emissions in any inventory and appear only
implicitly as sink changes. Richard is right that the retained stock has not
broken (≈32-yr doubling across the whole Mauna Loa record, growth rate still
rising decade on decade), but it is not more powerful than the pre-1970
emissions exponential, and the decadal airborne fraction shows no trend
through the 2010s, so the data do not yet show crippled sinks; the 2020–2025
partial decade is high but inflated by the 2023–24 El Niño. Offered three
editorial options, recommending keeping Figure 4 with a relabel plus one
sentence conceding the stock point, and flagged that forcing is logarithmic
in CO2 so "exponential" should not be read through to warming.
